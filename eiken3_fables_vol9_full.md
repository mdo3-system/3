## はじめに：なぜ「対比多読」で英検3級の壁を突破できるのか？

英検3級の合格を目指す英語学習者にとって、最初の大きな壁は**「単文の箇条書き」から「接続詞や関係代名詞を用いたまとまりのある英文」への飛躍**です。

4級までは短い平易な文（SVOや助動詞can/willなど）の組み合わせで対応できましたが、3級からは「when, because, if, so」などの従属接続詞や現在完了形、受動態、不定詞・動名詞を駆使した自然な英文読解・リスニング力が問われます。

本書では、世界中で愛読される珠玉のイソップ寓話（第3集・10話）を題材に、**「知っている4級英語」** が **「3級合格レベルの英語」** へとどのようにステップアップするかを分かりやすく対比学習します。

* **馴染みのあるストーリー（4級英文）** で文脈を瞬時に把握
* **洗練された表現（3級英文）** で接続詞・複文・重要熟語を自然にインプット
* **暗唱キーフレーズ対比** で面接スピーキング・英作文に直結する表現力を定着

楽しく名作寓話を読み進めながら、3級合格に必要な英語の基礎体力を固めましょう！

## 1. Benchmark Level: Eiken Grade 4 (英検4級レベル本文)
*Read in ~1 to 1.5 minutes. Baseline vocabulary and basic syntax.*

Once, a poor slave named Androcles ran away from his cruel master. He hid in a dark cave in the forest.

Soon, a big lion entered the cave. Androcles was terrified, but the lion did not attack him. The lion was crying in pain because a sharp thorn was stuck in his paw. 

Androcles approached the lion bravely and pulled out the thorn. The lion felt relieved and licked the man's hand like a dog. They lived together in the cave as friends.

A few months later, soldiers found Androcles and took him back to the city. The king decided to feed him to a hungry lion in a large stadium.

Many people came to watch. When the cage opened, a ferocious lion rushed toward Androcles. But when the lion reached him, it stopped. It recognized its old friend and began to rub its head against his legs affectionately.

The king was amazed by this wonderful sight. After he heard their story, the king freed both Androcles and the faithful lion.

Kindness is never forgotten.

## 2. Target Level: Eiken Grade 3 (英検3級レベル本文)
*Read in ~2 to 3 minutes. Upgraded vocabulary, sophisticated sentence structures, and idioms.*

A slave named Androcles once escaped from a harsh master who treated him poorly. While wandering through a vast forest, he discovered a quiet cave where he could hide safely.

Suddenly, a huge lion appeared at the entrance, groaning in agony. Although Androcles was trembling with fear, he noticed that the beast was limping. The lion held up its swollen paw, which had a large, painful thorn deeply embedded in it. Overcoming his fear, Androcles carefully extracted the thorn and cleaned the wound. Deeply grateful, the gentle lion purred and became his loyal companion.

However, soldiers eventually captured Androcles and condemned him to be thrown to the wild beasts in the royal arena. On the day of punishment, an enormous crowd gathered to witness the execution. A ferocious lion, which had been starved for days, was released into the ring. It roared fiercely and charged straight at Androcles.

Yet, as soon as the beast recognized the young man, it immediately knelt down and purred softly, licking his hands with affection. The emperor, who was astonished by this miraculous scene, called Androcles to explain. Moved by their touching bond, the emperor pardoned Androcles and granted total freedom to both the man and his loyal beast.

True gratitude knows no boundaries, and mercy is always rewarded.

## 3. Classic Original English Text (イソップ寓話 クラシック原文)
*Townsend / Jacobs Edition (Public Domain)*

A slave named Androcles once escaped from his master and fled into the forest. As he was wandering about there, he came upon a Lion lying down, groaning and moaning. At first he turned to flee, but finding that the Lion did not pursue him, he turned back and went up to him. As he came near, the Lion put out his paw, which was all swollen and bleeding, and Androcles found that a huge thorn had got into it, and was causing all the pain. He pulled out the thorn and bound up the paw of the Lion, who was soon able to rise and lick the hand of Androcles like a dog. Then the Lion took Androcles to his cave, and every day used to bring him meat from which the two lived. 

But shortly afterwards both Androcles and the Lion were captured, and the slave was sentenced to be thrown to the Lion, after the latter had been kept without food for several days. The Emperor and all his Court came to look on at the spectacle, and Androcles was led out into the middle of the arena. Soon the Lion was let loose from his den, and rushed bounding and roaring towards his victim. But as soon as he came near to Androcles he recognised his friend, and fawned upon him, and licked his hands like a friendly dog. The Emperor was astonished at this strange spectacle and summoned Androcles to him. Androcles told the Emperor all that had happened, and was at once pardoned and set free, while the Lion was also loosed into his native forest.

Gratitude is the sign of noble souls.

## 6. Key Vocabulary & Exam Notes (語彙・文法・試験対策解説)

### 【出題頻出語彙・熟語ノート】
- **escape from** [熟] 〜から逃亡する、脱出する（*escape from danger*）
- **wander** [動] さまよう、歩き回る（*wander through the woods*）
- **groan** [動] うめく、うめき声をあげる
- **limp** [動] 足を引きずって歩く
- **extract** [動] 〜を抜き取る、引き抜く（*pull out* のフォーマルな表現）
- **wound** [名] 傷、負傷（動詞としても頻出）
- **condemn** [動] 〜に刑を宣告する、非難する（*condemn A to B*）
- **witness** [動] 〜を目撃する、立ち会う（名詞：目撃者）
- **astonish** [動] 〜を大いに驚かせる（*be astonished by/at...*「〜に驚嘆する」）
- **gratitude** [名] 感謝の気持ち（形容詞 *grateful*）

### 【文法・構文ポイント】
1. **関係代名詞の目的格・主格修飾 [who / which]**
   - 3級本文: *"A slave named Androcles once escaped from a harsh master who treated him poorly."*
   - 3級本文: *"A ferocious lion, which had been starved for days, was released into the ring."*
   - ポイント: 先行詞が人なら `who`、動物や物なら `which` を用いて、後ろから詳しく説明を加える英検3級の最重要文法。
2. **[as soon as ...]（〜するやいなや）と受動態の完了 [had been + 過去分詞]**
   - 3級本文: *"Yet, as soon as the beast recognized the young man, it immediately knelt down..."*
   - ポイント: 「Aが〜するとすぐにBした」という時間的連続性を表す重要表現。過去の基準時点より前に起きた「何日も飢えさせられていた（had been starved）」という受動過去完了と合わせて、物語の臨場感を高めます。

## 1. Benchmark Level: Eiken Grade 4 (英検4級レベル本文)
*Read in ~1 to 1.5 minutes. Baseline vocabulary and basic syntax.*

One morning, a black crow found a delicious piece of cheese. She flew to a high branch of a tall tree to eat it quietly.

A sly fox was walking through the woods. He was very hungry, and he smelled the cheese. He looked up and saw the crow on the branch.

The fox wanted the cheese, so he decided to trick her. He walked to the tree and said politely, "Good morning, beautiful bird! Your black feathers are so shiny and wonderful! You look like the queen of birds."

The crow was very pleased to hear this praise. 

Then the fox said, "I am sure your voice is as sweet as your feathers. Please sing a song for me!"

The crow wanted to show her lovely voice. She opened her beak and cried loudly, "Caw! Caw!" 

At that moment, the cheese fell from her mouth. The fox caught it quickly and ate it all. 

"Thank you for the cheese," laughed the fox. "You have a voice, but you have no brains!"

Do not trust people who flatter you.

---
<div style="page-break-before: always;"></div>

## 2. Target Level: Eiken Grade 3 (英検3級レベル本文)
*Read in ~2 to 3 minutes. Upgraded vocabulary, sophisticated sentence structures, and idioms.*

A proud crow had successfully stolen a large piece of cheese from an open cottage window. Feeling thoroughly pleased with herself, she flew up to the safety of a high tree branch, intending to savor her prize.

A crafty fox, which had been prowling in search of a meal, caught the rich aroma of the cheese. He crept silently toward the base of the tree and gazed up at the bird. Knowing that he could not climb the tree, he realized that he had to use flattery to accomplish his goal.

"Greetings, magnificent creature!" the fox called out in a smooth, gentle tone. "How extraordinarily glossy your feathers look in the morning sunshine! Surely, a bird of such rare beauty must possess a voice that is equally enchanting. If only I could hear your melodious song, I would certainly bow to you as the true queen of all birds."

Flattered by these sweet compliments, the foolish crow completely lost her common sense. Eager to prove that she possessed a magnificent voice, she lifted her head, opened her beak wide, and let out a loud screech.

The instant her beak opened, the precious cheese dropped directly to the ground. The fox snapped it up immediately and swallowed it with delight. Looking up at the crestfallen bird, he chuckled, "Your singing is loud enough, my dear, but you sorely lack good judgment."

Never trust those who shower you with empty flattery.

## 3. Classic Original English Text (イソップ寓話 クラシック原文)
*Townsend / Jacobs Edition (Public Domain)*

A Crow had snatched a piece of cheese and flew up into a high tree with it to eat it at her leisure. A Fox, seeing this, resolved to possess himself of the cheese, and coming up to the foot of the tree, began to flatter the Crow. 

"What a noble bird I see before me!" he said. "Her beauty is without equal, the hue of her plumage exquisite. If only her voice were as sweet as her looks are fair, she ought without doubt to be the Queen of the Birds."

The Crow, hugely flattered by this speech, and wishing to show the Fox that she could sing, opened her mouth and gave a loud caw. The cheese fell, and the Fox, snatching it up, said, "My good Crow, your voice is right enough, but your wit is wanting."

Do not trust flatterers.

## 4. Japanese Translation (日本語訳)

ある誇り高いカラスが、開いていた小屋の窓から大きなチーズの塊を首尾よく奪い取りました。彼女はすっかり得意になり、獲物をじっくり味わおうと高い木の枝の安全な場所へと飛び立ちました。

食事を探してうろついていた悪賢いキツネが、チーズの豊かな香りに気づきました。キツネは木の下へと静かに忍び寄り、木の上の鳥を見上げました。木には登れないと分かっていたキツネは、目的を達成するためにはお世辞を使うしかないと悟りました。

「ごきげんよう、素晴らしいお方！」キツネは滑らかで優しい声で呼びかけました。「朝の陽光に輝くあなたの羽は、なんと見事に艶やかなのでしょう！これほどの類まれな美しさを持つ鳥なら、その声も同様に魅惑的であるに違いありません。もしあなたの美しい歌声を聴くことができたなら、私はすべての鳥の真の女王としてあなたにひれ伏すことでしょう」

この甘い賛辞におだてられた愚かなカラスは、すっかり分別を失ってしまいました。自分が見事な声を持っていることを証明したくてたまらなくなったカラスは、頭をもたげ、くちばしを大きく開いて、甲高い大声をあげました。

くちばしが開いた瞬間、貴重なチーズは真っ直ぐ地面へと落下しました。キツネはそれをすぐさまパクリとくわえ、大喜びで飲み込みました。がっくりとうなだれる鳥を見上げながら、キツネはくすくす笑いました。「お前の歌声は十分うるさいが、残念ながら正しい判断力にひどく欠けているようだな」

**教訓**: 口先ばかりのお世辞を浴びせてくる者を決して信じてはならない。

### 【文法・構文ポイント】
1. **関係代名詞の継続用法・限定用法 [which / that]**
   - 3級本文: *"A crafty fox, which had been prowling in search of a meal, caught the rich aroma of the cheese."*
   - 3級本文: *"Surely, a bird of such rare beauty must possess a voice that is equally enchanting."*
   - ポイント: 先行詞（fox や voice）を関係代名詞でつなぎ、情景や性質を1文の中で豊かに肉付けする表現。長文読解で文の骨格を見抜くカギとなります。

   - ポイント: 話の起点となる時点よりも前に起こった出来事を表す「過去完了（had + 過去分詞）」は、物語の背景状況を読者に提示する定番の手法です。

- **resolve** [動] 〜を決意する（*resolve to do...*）
- **imitate** [動] 〜を模倣する、真似る（名詞 *imitation*）
- **reckless** [形] 向こう見ずな、無謀な（副詞 *recklessly*）
- **entangle** [動] 〜をもつれさせる、巻き込む（*be entangled in...*）
- **to make matters worse** [熟] さらに悪いことには（*what is worse*）
- **witness** [動] 〜を目撃する（*witness an accident*）
- **judge by** [熟] 〜によって判断する（*judge a person by appearance*）
- **merely** [副] 単に〜にすぎない（*only / just* の改まった表現）
- **lead to** [熟] 〜につながる、〜を引き起こす（*lead to success/failure*）

### 【文法・構文ポイント】
1. **独立不定詞 [To make matters worse]（さらに悪いことには）**
   - 3級本文: *"To make matters worse, his sharp claws became hopelessly entangled..."*
   - ポイント: 文頭に置いて文全体を修飾する定型表現。3級の読解・リスニングで事態の暗転を示す合図として頻出します。
2. **動名詞による主語表現 [V-ing ...] と前置詞句**
   - 3級本文: *"Imitating the powerful without possessing their strength will inevitably lead to humiliation..."*
   - ポイント: `Imitating ...`（真似すること）が文全体の主語となり、`will lead to` が述語動詞。前置詞 `without` の後ろにも動名詞 `possessing` が続く構造は、英検ライティングで高得点を獲得する模範構文です。

## 6. Key Vocabulary & Exam Notes (語彙・文法・試験対策解説)

### 【出題頻出語彙・熟語ノート】
- **refuge** [名] 避難所、保護（*seek refuge*「避難する」）
- **plead** [動] 嘆願する、懇願する（*plead for mercy*）
- **conceal** [動] 〜を隠す、秘密にする（*hide* のフォーマル語）
- **happen to do** [熟] 偶然〜する（*happen to see...*）
- **gesture** [動] 身振り手振りで合図する（名詞：身振り）
- **betray** [動] 〜を裏切る、（秘密など）を漏らす
- **fail to do** [構] 〜し損ねる、〜できない（*not* の洗練された表現）
- **subtle** [形] 微妙な、かすかな、目立たない
- **rebuke** [動] 〜を強く非難する、叱責する
- **contradict** [動] 〜と矛盾する、反対のことを言う

### 【文法・構文ポイント】
1. **関係代名詞の所有格 [whose + 名詞]**
   - 3級本文: *"Never trust individuals whose actions directly contradict their spoken promises."*
   - ポイント: 「その人の（所有）〜が…である」を表す所有格の関係代名詞。英検3級〜準2級の文法問題で頻出するハイレベル構文です。
2. **否定を婉曲に表す [fail to do ...]（〜できない、〜し忘れる）**
   - 3級本文: *"Fortunately, the eager hunters failed to notice his subtle hand gestures..."*
   - ポイント: 単に `did not notice` と言うよりも、「気づくべきところに気づかなかった」というニュアンスを的確に伝える表現です。

## 1. Benchmark Level: Eiken Grade 4 (英検4級レベル本文)
*Read in ~1 to 1.5 minutes. Baseline vocabulary and basic syntax.*

A small lamb was drinking water at a quiet stream. 

A hungry wolf walked to the stream. He saw the gentle lamb and wanted to eat her. However, he wanted an excuse to attack her.

The wolf walked upstream and shouted angrily, "Why are you making my drinking water dirty?"

The poor lamb looked up and said gently, "Sir, the water flows from you down to me. I cannot make your water dirty."

The wolf had nothing to say to that, so he tried another excuse. "Last year, you said terrible things about me!"

The lamb replied, "Sir, I was only born this spring. I was not even alive last year!"

The wolf became furious. "If it was not you, it must be your father!" he roared.

A wicked person will always find an excuse to do harm.

## 2. Target Level: Eiken Grade 3 (英検3級レベル本文)
*Read in ~2 to 3 minutes. Upgraded vocabulary, sophisticated sentence structures, and idioms.*

A little lamb was peacefully quenching its thirst beside a clear brook. Further upstream, a ravenous wolf emerged from the forest in search of prey. Desiring to devour the tender animal, yet wishing to justify his cruelty with a plausible excuse, the wolf approached with a fierce scowl.

"How dare you muddy the clean water that I am trying to drink?" the wolf snarled menacingly.

The frightened lamb replied in a meek tone, "Excuse me, noble sir, but the current flows downward from your position to mine. It is completely impossible for me to disturb the water upstream."

Finding his first accusation easily disproved by logic, the wolf quickly invented another complaint. "Last year, you insulted my character with slanderous words!"

"That cannot be true," pleaded the lamb, trembling with fear, "for I was not even born last year."

Realizing that reason could not defeat truth, the tyrant dropped all pretenses of fairness. "If it was not you, then it was certainly your father!" roared the wolf. Without waiting for any reply, he seized the defenseless creature and devoured it instantly.

Tyrants will always devise excuses for cruelty, and innocence is no protection against brutal force.
